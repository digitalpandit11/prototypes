1Executive summary

BPositive will sell to two very different kinds of buyer through two separate front-ends:

B2C storefront Consumers

  • Typical e-commerce UI: browse, cart, checkout
  • Uniform pricing: every consumer sees the same price
  • Pays upfront via payment gateway or chooses Cash on Delivery

B2B portal Businesses

  • Separate, login-only UI built for bulk and repeat ordering
  • Differential pricing: each business has its own price list
  • Payment terms per business: Advance, COD or Credit of N days

After an order is placed, both channels converge on one admin panel. The BPositive team physically picks the material, makes it ready for dispatch, selects a shipping partner from the integrated (API-wired) couriers and sends a draft bill to the buyer. A B2C buyer has normally already paid (or pays the courier on delivery). A B2B buyer usually pays later, either online or by NEFT, and then records the payment reference on the portal so the team can reconcile it.

4Pricing model

B2C: uniform pricing

Each product has one MRP and one selling price. All consumers see the same price. Promotions (coupons, sale prices) apply to everyone equally and are managed centrally.

B2B: price list per business

Each business account is linked to a price list. The portal shows that business only its own prices. Several businesses can share a list (e.g. "Dealer – Tier 1"), or one business can have a dedicated contract list.

How a B2B price is resolved

When a logged-in business views a product, the system picks the first price that applies:

  1. Contract price for this business & SKUA negotiated rate agreed with that specific business, if one exists.
  2. Price-list priceThe rate from the price list / tier assigned to the business.
  3. Volume slabQuantity-based rates (e.g. 1–49, 50–199, 200+ units) applied on top, if configured.
  4. Fallback: base B2B rateA default trade price when nothing more specific is set.
Every price list has a validity period, so rates can be revised without affecting orders already placed. An order always stores the price that was applied at the moment it was placed.

5Payment terms & checkout logic

Advance

Business pays the full amount (online or NEFT) before the order is released for picking.

COD

Business pays the courier on delivery. Usually limited by order value and serviceable pincodes.

Credit (N days)

Order ships immediately. Payment is due N days from the bill date (e.g. 7, 15, 30). Subject to a credit limit.

Advance orders stay in "Awaiting payment" and are not released to the warehouse until the payment is confirmed. Credit orders are released immediately but count against the business's available credit (credit limit − outstanding unpaid bills).

7Fulfilment & shipping

Step by step in the admin panel

  1. New order notificationThe order appears in the admin panel queue, tagged B2C or B2B, with payment status visible. Advance orders without payment stay on hold.
  2. Confirm & check stockThe team confirms the order. If an item is short, they can part-ship, back-order or edit the quantity (the buyer is informed).
  3. Pick listThe system prints a pick list (item, quantity, storage location). The team physically picks the material and ticks items off.
  4. Pack & weighThe team packs the material and enters the actual weight and box dimensions. These drive the courier rates.
  5. Mark "Ready to Ship"The order is now available for dispatch.
  6. Select shipping partnerThe admin panel calls the integrated courier APIs and shows each partner's rate, estimated delivery date and COD availability for that pincode. The team picks one (or accepts the system's recommendation: cheapest / fastest).
  7. Book shipmentThe courier API returns an AWB / tracking number and a printable shipping label. Pickup is scheduled.
  8. TrackTracking updates flow back via the courier's API or webhooks (Shipped → In transit → Out for delivery → Delivered / RTO). The buyer gets the tracking link.

8Draft bill & payment collection

Once the material is packed and the shipping partner is fixed, the system has the final quantities and freight cost. It generates a draft bill and sends it to the buyer (email, SMS / WhatsApp link, and on the portal).

B2C collection

  • Gateway orders: already paid at checkout. The bill is marked Paid; the gateway settlement is matched later.
  • COD orders: the courier collects cash / UPI at the door and remits it to BPositive. The admin panel matches the courier's COD remittance report against the orders.

B2B collection

  • Advance: paid before picking; bill is marked Paid.
  • COD: same as B2C COD, via courier remittance.
  • Credit: bill is Due with a due date. The buyer pays later in one of two ways (below).

B2B pay-later: two routes

RouteWhat the buyer doesWhat BPositive does
OnlineClicks "Pay now" on the bill in the portal (or the payment link in the email) and pays through the gateway.Nothing manual: the gateway confirms the payment and the bill is marked Paid automatically.
NEFT / RTGSTransfers the money from their bank, then on the portal selects the bill(s), enters amount, date and UTR / reference number, and optionally uploads the bank advice.Finance checks the bank statement for that UTR, then approves (bill → Paid or Part-paid) or rejects with a reason.
Reminders & control: automatic reminders before and after the due date; overdue bills reduce available credit and, past a set number of days, block new credit orders until cleared. One NEFT payment can be allocated across several bills.

Procurement

How material comes in: into BPositive's stores, and into the B2B buyer's premises.

12Procurement: two sides

Procurement on this platform has two separate meanings, and the platform supports both:

A · BPositive's own procurement

BPositive buys stock from its suppliers to sell on the B2C and B2B channels. That buying happens offline today and inventory is not held in any live system. Phase 1 gives the team a simple screen to record physical stock as it arrives, with the buying rate. Phase 2 adds an intelligent vendor finder to place orders.

B · The B2B buyer's procurement

For a business customer, an order on the B2B portal is their purchase. The portal lets them receive material against the order, line by line, so any short, damaged or wrong delivery is caught and reported to BPositive immediately.

13BPositive stock inward Phase 1

Inventory is not online today, so Phase 1 does not try to automate purchasing. It gives the store team one simple screen to record what physically came in and what it cost. That is enough to show correct stock on both storefronts and to know the margin on every sale.

How it works

  1. Material arrivesThe team buys as they do today and the material reaches the store with the supplier's bill.
  2. Stock Inward entryThe team opens Admin → Stock Inward and enters the supplier, bill number and date, then one line per item: quantity received and buying rate (plus GST %). The supplier bill can be photographed and attached.
  3. Stock and cost updateSaving adds the quantity to stock and recalculates the item's average buying cost. The item becomes sellable on the B2C store and the B2B portal.
  4. Corrections & physical countA Stock Adjustment screen handles damage, loss and count differences, each with a reason. A periodic Physical Count sheet lets the team enter actual counts and see differences before posting them.

Costing: the item's average cost is recalculated on every inward entry: (old stock × old avg cost + new qty × new rate) ÷ total qty. The admin panel then shows margin per order and per item. Selling prices stay separate: they come from the B2C price and the B2B price lists (Part 1, §4).
Stock movement on sales: stock is reserved when an order is confirmed and deducted when it is marked Ready to Ship. Cancellations and RTO returns put it back.

14Intelligent vendor finder Phase 2

Once Phase 1 has collected a few months of buying history (who supplied what, at what rate, how quickly), the platform can use it to suggest the best vendor for each purchase and place the order from the admin panel.

15B2B buyer: receive material against order

When a B2B shipment is delivered, the buyer confirms receipt against that order on the portal, item by item. Differences are captured while the courier's delivery is still fresh, with photos, and reach the BPositive team instantly instead of surfacing weeks later as a payment dispute.

Rules

  • Reporting window: the buyer has a fixed window after delivery (e.g. 48 hours) to receive the order. If they do nothing, the order is auto-marked Received.
  • Partial receipt: if an order arrives in more than one shipment, each shipment is received separately against the same order.
  • Payment link: while a discrepancy is open, the disputed amount is held. The undisputed part of the bill stays due on its normal date.
